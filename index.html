    Motors and weight

    Motor current figures

    Typical figures, not measured on your motors: VEX publishes the stall torque (2.1 N m for the 11 W motor on the 100 rpm cartridge, 0.5 N m for the 5.5 W motor) and the 2.5 A limit; the true stall and free current (about 4.7 A and 0.14 A for the 11 W motor) come from a community fit on the VEX Forum. The 5.5 W motor is not exactly half of an 11 W one, so it has its own figures.

    The wheel RPM above is the wheel's ideal speed (cartridge x gearing). With this on, the speed and response are worked out from the motors' torque, the current limit, the gearing, the weight and the rolling resistance of your wheels, and the two boxes above show the result. Mix 11 W and 5.5 W motors per side to match your drive (two 11 W and one 5.5 W a side is a 55 W drive, 27.5 W a side).

    Chassis wheels + tracking wheels

    Front of the robot is up. There are 4 wheel positions a side (8 in all). Pick Omni, Traction or Blank for each, or click a wheel in the drawing to step through them. A 4WD is just four blanks. A blank position has no wheel. Wheel size is the wheel diameter above; the robot length and track width set where the wheels sit.

    Goal aligners hit the goals

    Off by default. It switches itself on while any aligners are on, because aligners need solid goals to push against. This is separate from the real-life error below: a perfect robot can still hit a goal.

    Goal base, flat to flat (in)

    The goals are screwed to the field, so they never move: the robot (its body and its aligners) is stopped by them, slides along them with friction and is turned by them. A goal counts as aligned when any part of it gets inside a small circle around the aligner's alignment point (the green diamond in the aligner editor). The circle's radius is Aligned within (in): 0 means the goal must touch the point, a larger number is more forgiving. With real-life error on, the aligners grip a little harder and vary from run to run, so aligning is slightly less certain than in a perfect sim. The goal footprint is a square with cut-off corners, measured off the field picture (about 5.65 in across, corners cut by 1 in); you can change it here.

    Real-life error gyro drift + wheel slip

    Error size scales every error at once: 1 is a typical robot, 2 is twice the gyro drift, noise and wheel slip with less grip (a worst case), 0.5 is half.

    On: every Run gives the robot a new random draw (its gyro has a bias, a turn-rate error and noise; the floor grip changes a little), so results vary from run to run like on a real field. How big the errors are comes from the Chassis above: traction wheels grip and slide less, tracking wheels keep the odometry right when the drive wheels slip. Changing a setting re-runs with the same draw. The odometry only sees the wheels and the gyro, so it slowly disagrees with where the robot really is. setPose() resets it, like a wall reset. Off (the default): a perfect robot, no odometry error and no slip.

          Goal aligners

          Keys: V select, L line, A 3-point arc, C centre arc, O circle, Delete, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y. Mouse wheel zooms, right or middle drag (or Alt + drag) pans. Everything is in inches from the robot's centre, front is up. Drag the green diamond to place the alignment point yourself.

          Auto-tune timeouts

          Runs your auton to see how long every motion really takes, then gives each call a timeout of that time plus a safety margin. A move that runs into a wall or a goal and can never get there gets a timeout a little after it hit (or after an aligner caught the goal), so it has time to square up but does not push in for long. It then runs the result again to check, and puts the numbers back into a copy of your code.
